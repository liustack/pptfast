# Data Practice Maturity Assessment

You're presenting the results of a data-practice assessment to the leadership team. The
consulting review sorted how a department handles data into four rungs of sophistication, and
every department in the company sits somewhere on this ladder today.

Rung one, the lowest: Ad Hoc — decisions run on spreadsheets someone maintains alone, nobody owns
data quality, numbers get re-derived differently by whoever needs them that week. Rung two:
Repeatable — a few shared dashboards exist, but someone still has to manually refresh and sanity-
check them before a meeting. Rung three: Managed — data lives in one governed warehouse with clear
ownership, and dashboards update themselves. Rung four, the top: Data-Led — teams can point to a
real decision they made last month that the data itself drove, and that habit is now normal, not
an exception.

The message leadership needs to land: a team can't skip a rung. You don't jump from Ad Hoc to
Data-Led — each level is built directly on top of the one below it, so the slide needs to show the
levels stacked in that bottom-to-top order, not just listed. Once the ladder itself is clear, place
three specific departments where they actually sit today: Finance is solidly Managed, Marketing is
still Repeatable, and Sales is Ad Hoc despite being the most revenue-critical team in the building.
Don't attach percentages or counts to the rungs themselves — the point is the shape of the ladder
and where each team currently stands on it, not what fraction of anything falls into each level.
